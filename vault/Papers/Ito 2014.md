# Do Consumers Respond to Marginal or Average Price? Evidence from Nonlinear Electricity Pricing

**Ito · 2014 · AER**
[↗ Full paper](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.104.2.537)
**Section:** Tax Misperceptions

## Abstract

Exploits discontinuities in tiered electricity pricing to test whether consumers respond to marginal or average price. Finds strong evidence of average-price responses: consumers appear to pay their average rate, not the marginal rate they face on the next unit. This systematic misperception of nonlinear pricing implies large welfare losses and has direct implications for the design of environmental taxes and utility rate structures.
